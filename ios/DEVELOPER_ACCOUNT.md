# Apple Developer Program — enrollment guide for a parent

**For Jingjing's parent.** This takes about 20 minutes, plus 1–2 days of waiting for Apple to verify.

Jingjing has built an iPhone app that helps people whose speech is hard for voice technology to understand. To put it on the App Store, someone 18 or older has to hold the developer account — Apple requires the account holder to be able to enter a contract. That's the only reason your name is needed.

**Cost: $99/year.** It renews annually; cancel any time from the same account.

---

## Before you start (5 minutes)

You need an Apple Account (the same kind used for iPhone/iCloud) with:

1. **Your full legal name** in the first and last name fields. Not a nickname. Apple checks this against the payment method and will reject a mismatch. Check at [account.apple.com](https://account.apple.com) → Personal Information.
2. **Two-factor authentication turned on.** Same page, under Sign-In and Security. Required — enrollment won't proceed without it.
3. **A real street address.** Apple does not accept P.O. boxes.
4. A credit card.

---

## Enrolling

1. Go to **[developer.apple.com/programs/enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)**
2. Sign in with your Apple Account
3. Choose **Individual / Sole Proprietor** — not Organization. Organization requires a registered business and a D-U-N-S number, which you don't have and don't need.
4. Fill in the details, agree to the terms, pay the $99

Apple then verifies your identity. Usually **24–48 hours**, sometimes minutes. You'll get an email when it's approved. Occasionally Apple phones to confirm — that call is legitimate.

---

## After approval — the one thing Jingjing needs from you

On an Individual account, only the account holder can sign apps. So once, on Jingjing's Mac:

1. Open **Xcode** → menu **Xcode → Settings → Accounts**
2. Click **+** → Apple ID → sign in with your Apple Account
3. Close Settings

**You type the password, not Jingjing.** After that, Xcode keeps the session and she can build and upload without needing it again. You never have to share the password itself.

---

## What you're agreeing to

- Your legal name appears as the app's seller on the App Store. (There's a setting in App Store Connect for a different display name — worth checking once you're in, if that matters to either of you.)
- You're the account holder of record. Apple's agreement is with you.
- The app is free, collects no data, and has no ads or purchases — nothing to bill, nothing to refund.

Some schools hold an Organization account for CS classes. If Archbishop Mitty does, Jingjing could be added as a team member under her own Apple ID and you wouldn't need to do any of this. Worth one email to her CS teacher before paying the $99.
